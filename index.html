      Three ways to read the heat

      Chart — a real cell from the table US Soccer publishes and Heartland prints at the bottom of their extreme-heat page. Find the air temperature along the top, the humidity down the side, read off the box. This is the number your coach and the other parents are quoting, so it is what this page shows by default.

      One catch worth knowing, because it will explain any argument you have with someone else’s lookup. The table only prints every 1.8°F and every 5% humidity, so a real forecast almost never lands exactly on a box. This page treats every printed value as a threshold: the reading has to actually reach the next column or row before it moves up, and it never rounds up. A 96°F forecast has not reached the 96.8 column, so it reads in the 95.0 column; 38% humidity has not reached the 40 row, so it reads in the 35 row. That box says 89.6 — heat schedule. Round to the nearest box instead and the same hour reads 93.2, a whole category up and cancelled. Across a typical week the choice moves the verdict in roughly one hour in three, always downward, so this page reads a little cooler than one that rounds to nearest. That is deliberate: it is the box a person actually points at when they run their finger across the printed table.

      Formula — the equation that table was generated from, run directly instead of looked up. The printed chart steps in 1.8° jumps because it is really a Celsius table; the formula gives the value in between. It agrees with every one of the 432 printed cells to within 1°.

      NWS model — the National Weather Service’s own hourly WBGT forecast for that exact field, the same product weather.gov publishes. This is the one that computes wet bulb globe temperature the way it is actually defined:

      WBGT = 0.7·Tnwb + 0.2·Tg + 0.1·Tdb

      Natural wet bulb, black globe, dry bulb. NOAA builds all three from forecast temperature, dew point, wind speed, sky cover and a solar radiation flux computed for that hour, date and latitude. The chart has only two of those inputs — air temperature and humidity — and approximates everything else. That one difference is the whole reason the two numbers disagree.

      The chart and the model routinely disagree, and the chart’s own authors say why. The table traces to Australia’s Bureau of Meteorology, who state that the approximation “does not take into account variations in the intensity of solar radiation or of windspeed” and that in cloudy or windy conditions it is “likely to lead to an overestimate of the stress.”

      Read “overestimate” carefully: wind does not push the chart’s number up. The chart cannot see wind at all — give it the same temperature and humidity on a still day and a blustery one and it returns the identical box. What moves is reality. A WBGT meter is mostly a wet thermometer and a black ball, and wind cools both, so the true reading falls while the chart stays put. The gap opens underneath it.

      In practice the chart routinely runs 5–9° above the model at every playing hour of the day — morning included — easily enough to put them in different categories all day. When they split, treat the day as genuinely borderline: the chart is the cautious number and the one that gets quoted; the model is closer to what a real meter at the field would read.

      Provenance: the table comes from the US Soccer Heat Guidelines, which adapted it from the Bureau of Meteorology, who in turn cite the American College of Sports Medicine’s 1984 position stand on preventing thermal injuries in distance running (Med. J. Aust., Dec 1984). The model comes from the NDFD wet bulb globe temperature algorithm (NOAA/NWS Meteorological Development Laboratory), operational since 2022. The chart’s own formula is WBGT = 0.567·Ta + 0.393·e + 3.94. Above about 90°F with high humidity the printed table simply stops, and this page falls back to the formula.

      Heartland’s three outcomes

      ≤ 86.9°Games on as scheduled
      87.0° – 91.9°Heat Schedule: shortened games + water breaks
      ≥ 92.0°Games suspended or cancelled

      This is a forecast, not the official call. Heartland reads an actual Wet Bulb Globe Temperature device on-site at game time and posts updates to their weather line. Use this to decide what to pack and what to expect — not whether a game is on.

      WBGT is not the same as plain wet-bulb temperature: it also folds in sunshine and wind, and runs roughly 10–15° hotter on a clear afternoon. Both numbers on this page are WBGT — the same quantity Heartland measures — just estimated two different ways. See “Why two numbers?” above.
